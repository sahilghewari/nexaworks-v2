You launch an AI support agent. For the first two weeks, it performs flawlessly. Resolution times drop, customers are happy, and your support team can finally breathe.

Then, in week three, it tells a highly valued Enterprise customer that your platform doesn't support SSO, even though your engineering team shipped SSO five days ago. 

The AI isn't broken. It's suffering from **Knowledge Drift**.

## What is Knowledge Drift?

Knowledge drift is the delta between your company's *actual* operational reality (what your product does today, what your current policies are) and the *documented* reality that your AI agent relies on.

In fast-moving B2B SaaS companies, knowledge is incredibly volatile. Decisions are made in Slack threads, feature specs are updated in Notion, and bug statuses change in Jira. 

If your AI is only reading your public-facing Help Center, it is always days—or weeks—behind the truth.

## Why Knowledge Drift Causes Hallucinations

When a user asks a question about a newly changed feature, the AI searches its vector database. Because the database hasn't been updated with the new reality, the AI finds the *old* policy. 

To the AI, this old policy is the absolute truth. It confidently relays this incorrect information to the user. This is the most dangerous type of hallucination, because it sounds completely plausible and cites official (albeit outdated) company documentation.

## How to Fix Knowledge Drift

Fixing knowledge drift isn't about switching to a better LLM like GPT-4 or Claude. It's an infrastructure problem.

### 1. Shift from Static Docs to Operational Memory
You must stop thinking of your knowledge base as a static repository of "Help Articles." Instead, you need an **Operational Memory Layer**. This layer connects directly to the heartbeat of your company: your Slack channels, your Jira boards, and your internal Notion workspaces.

### 2. Continuous Re-indexing
A weekly sync is not enough. Your AI pipeline needs to be aware of changes in near real-time. When a Product Manager updates a spec in Notion, that change should instantly propagate through your vector embeddings. 

### 3. Source Attribution and Decay Metrics
Your system should assign a "confidence score" to information based on its age and engagement. A Slack message from 2022 stating that "we will never support SOC2" should have a much lower confidence score than a Notion document from yesterday titled "SOC2 Compliance Framework."
